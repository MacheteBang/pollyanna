D · Tablecloth

The kitchen table instead of the desk. A blue gingham cloth is the one pattern: your event sits on it like a place set for you, and the gift is wrapped in it. Everything else is white and ink.

The one bold thing
The gingham, used in two places only: behind your event on home, and around the gift.
Homey how
Cloth, not paper. The name appears in a tidy hand.
Type
Figtree, with Playpen Sans for the revealed name only.
Main risk
Gingham can tip into picnic or country-kitchen kitsch, and it is hard to carry into email, where it would shrink to a plain blue band.

E · Around the table

The family is the decoration. Everyone taking part is a seat at a round table under warm lamp-yellow light; the gift sits in the middle, and untying it lights up the person you got.

The one bold thing
The ring of people. It is the only picture besides the bow and girl, and it is made of real information: who is in this with you.
Homey how
Lamp light and faces at a table. No handwriting at all, so nothing to worry about for legibility.
Type
Gabarito, round and sturdy.
Main risk
Initials in circles are a familiar app pattern, and a ring gets crowded past about 14 people. It also shows the people before the reveal, which the screen order decided earlier keeps collapsed.

F · Plain and kind

Almost nothing on the page but the girl, the bow and a sentence that talks to you. The system speaks in plain type; anything a person wrote (their name, their notes) appears in a tidy hand, so the people are the warm part.

The one bold thing
The marks you already like, given room: the girl large on home, the bow large at the reveal.
Homey how
Words that sound like a relative, and family members' own words in handwriting.
Type
Hanken Grotesk, with Playpen Sans for people's own words.
Main risk
The quietest of all six. On a screen with no drawing and nothing handwritten it may read as plain rather than warm.

A · One ribbon

One hot color, ink, and a single line of cord. Your events hang on the cord in date order; at the reveal you pull the bow out and the ribbon lies flat over the name.

The one bold thing
The cord. Orange marks only what is waiting on you, and the gift itself.
Paper metaphor
Gone. Flat color, square corners, no texture or tilt.
Type
Figtree for everything, heavy for names and actions.
Main risk
The nearest to an ordinary app. It leans on the orange, the cord and the wordmark to be its own thing.

B · Ink and bows

A cool white page, a loose ink line, and color only inside the bows. The girl stands at the top of the page looking in at your exchanges; the name arrives in handwriting.

The one bold thing
The drawing. Everything else is type on a bare page with no boxes.
Paper metaphor
A trace of it: ink on a page, and the reveal is a folded note. No kraft, stamps, tears or tilt.
Type
Young Serif for names and headings, Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for reading, Shantell Sans for the revealed name only.
Main risk
It needs a real hand. The wobble here is a filter standing in for one, and a weak line looks worse than none. A serif with a warm accent can also drift toward the look §12 bans, so the page stays cool and the bows stay raspberry.

C · Cut shapes

Three flat colors and big rounded blocks. The bow is two wedges and a dot; the girl is built from the same few shapes. Each event is a block you could pick up, and the gift is the biggest block of all.

The one bold thing
Scale: huge names, huge countdown numbers, a bow the size of your thumb.
Paper metaphor
Gone as a material, though flat cut shapes still feel a little like craft paper.
Type
Bricolage Grotesque for everything, very heavy at large sizes.
Main risk
The busiest of the three, and it brings back a stack of cards. Bright rounded shapes are widely copied, and three colors can quickly become five.
