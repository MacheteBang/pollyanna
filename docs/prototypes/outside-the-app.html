Six picks for the owner

  1. Email layout. A, the panel on the band (recommended), B, a band across the top, or C, a letter with no band.
  2. No image in the email. The spec's "nothing remote, no images" rule (§15) is one of the fixed rules, so the email carries the wordmark as text and no bow. The earlier note about a bow image on the band then falls away. The switch shows what a bow would add; keeping the rule is recommended.
  3. Touch icon. A, the bow tied on the cloth (recommended), B, the bow on a white panel, or C, the girl on a white panel.
  4. README banner. A, the wordmark panel on the cloth (recommended), B, the tied gift with a tag, or C, mostly white with a strip of cloth.
  5. Social preview. The same composition as the banner at 1280 × 640, with or without a line of words (recommended: without).
  6. Screenshots. A, three phones in a row (recommended), or B, one wide shot beside one phone as today. Either way the set is home, the event tied, the event untied, organizing, questions, and a GIF of the untying.

The email

  • Order as decided: event name, the kind's text, the button, the address small under it, the footer. No status stamp.
  • Six colors only, all solid. The pale-blue band stands in for the cloth; text never sits on it in A.
  • System font, since an email loads nothing: San Francisco on Apple, Segoe UI on Windows, Roboto on Android. The wordmark is lowercase bold text.
  • One 10px corner on the panel and the button. Classic Outlook squares both, and nothing is lost.
  • Sentence-case button, replacing today's spaced capitals.
  • Darkened: every surface sets its own background, so a client that inverts the email swaps whole surfaces and text stays readable. The band becomes a dark blue-gray. With no image there is nothing that fails to invert.
  • The three "slip" phrases use the new words.

Touch icon and favicon

All three at home-screen size, 60px, where the choice matters most.

The favicon is already decided: the bow without folds. Shown for reference on a light and a dark browser tab. On the dark tab the ink knot sinks into the background and the loops carry the shape.

  • The cloth on the icon uses six checks across, so the pattern still reads as gingham at 60px. At the app's 14px check it would turn to gray noise.
  • A is the gift from the reveal: bow on cloth with the white edge. It gives the bow the most room. It stretches the rule that the white edge appears only at the reveal.
  • B keeps the rule that only a white panel sits on the cloth, and costs the bow about a quarter of its size.
  • C puts the girl on the home screen. She stays above her 32px floor, but her braids and bows blur at 60px.

README banner

  • 1280 × 320 as today, shown at the README's 820px. The wordmark is Figtree 800 in lowercase, converted to outlines in the real file because GitHub loads no fonts inside an SVG.
  • The banner stays light on GitHub's dark theme, like the app. A and B fill their own rectangle; C has a white field that sits as a bright block on dark.
  • The line under the banner is today's, kept as a placeholder. It says "names in a hat", which describes the tradition, so the words ticket lets it stand; /to-spec can revisit it with the README's alt texts.

Social preview

  • 1280 × 640, the size GitHub asks for, shown as a link card. The site that shows the card prints the repository name and description beside it, so a line inside the image says it twice.
  • It follows the banner's composition here; the select lets you compare a different one.
  • The owner uploads it in the repository settings; it is a PNG kept in docs/assets/.
