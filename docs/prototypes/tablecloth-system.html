The tablecloth system

Every token for the new look on one page. Six things are still yours to pick; they are the controls in the bar at the bottom, and the whole sheet redraws when you change one. It opens on what I recommend.

In use

Color

Six colors. White and ink do nearly all the work; blue belongs to the bows and the cloth; red appears only when something is wrong or can't be undone.

The gingham your pick

Two layers of ribbon-blue thread on white, so a crossing comes out darker than a single thread, as on woven cloth. It is drawn in CSS, with no image file.

Where it appears

  • Once per screen, as a full-width band under the one white panel that holds what is yours to do.
  • Around the gift at the reveal.
  • On the touch icon, the README banner and the social preview, behind the mark.

Where it never appears

  • Behind text, links or form fields.
  • As a border, divider, button fill or page background.
  • In email, which gets a plain pale-blue band instead.
  • On a screen with nothing for you to do (past events, account, errors): those stay white.

Type your pick

One face for everything, in three weights, and a handwriting face used for one thing: the name you are giving to. Both are open-licensed (SIL OFL 1.1) and would be served by the app.

Space and corners

Spacing steps

481216243256

16 is the page gutter, 24 the padding inside a panel, 56 the cloth showing below a panel. Anything you can tap is at least 44px tall.

One corner

10px on panels, buttons and fields. Nothing is fully rounded and nothing has a shadow. Lines are 2px: ink around fields and outlined buttons, pale blue between rows.

Wordmark your pick

The girl at 40px, then the name in the text face at weight 800, tightened by 0.02em. No separate logo drawing. The mockups set it in lowercase; the app today capitalizes it.

The bow and the girl

Both stay as drawn. The girl's kraft-colored gaps take the color of whatever she stands on, and her shirt becomes pale blue on white, or white on pale blue. The bow keeps ribbon-blue loops, ink folds at 30% and an ink knot; at 16px the folds are left out, as in today's favicon.

Statuses your pick

Draft, open, names drawn, revealed, archived. They are states, so none of them gets a color. The two sample screens at the top show whichever treatment is picked.

Words, with steps for the organizer (recommended)

A participant reads the status as plain words in the small line under the date. The organizing page adds the row of steps, with the current one in ink over a blue line, because there the order is real and the next step is the organizer's to take.

Words only

The status is always a phrase inside a sentence, never an object of its own. The quietest option; the organizer loses the at-a-glance view of what comes next.

Tags

A small outlined label next to the title. Familiar, but it is the old stamp in new clothes, it breaks the one-corner rule, and it names a state without saying what to do.

What I need from you

  1. Check size: 10, 14 or 20px. I recommend 14.
  2. Check strength: light, medium or strong. I recommend medium.
  3. Text face: Figtree, Atkinson Hyperlegible Next or Hanken Grotesk. I recommend Figtree.
  4. Hand for the revealed name: Playpen Sans or Shantell Sans. I recommend Playpen Sans.
  5. Statuses: words with steps for the organizer, words only, or tags. I recommend the first.
  6. Wordmark: lowercase or capitalized. I recommend lowercase.
  7. Red: keep it for errors and "Cancel this event" only, with no red anywhere else (unread questions are shown by position and words, not a red dot).
  8. The girl's 32px floor and the rule that neither mark sits directly on the cloth.
