# Modern but warm identities worth learning from

Research for issue #52 (part of map #50, "A new visual identity"). Researched November 26, 2026.
The question: which existing identities feel modern and uncluttered while evoking warmth,
family and friendship, and by what means (color, type, illustration, shape, motion, words)?

This is references and observations only. Choosing a direction belongs to the later tickets
and to the owner. Nothing here changes SPEC.md.

## What is being replaced, and what any direction must keep

SPEC.md §12 ("Paper slips") is a table with a gift on it: a kraft ground, torn slips set at
slight angles, rubber stamps for statuses, passed notes for questions, Courier Prime for
everything and a pencil hand for the revealed name. Map #50 records that the owner finds it
cluttered and not intuitive.

Every direction keeps the two motifs (a bow, and a girl seen from behind with bows in her
hair), must not look tied to one holiday, uses only open-licensed fonts and images served by
the app, loads nothing remote, and meets WCAG AA contrast.

## Sources and how far to trust them

Each entry below says which kind of source backs it:

- **Studio or first-party page, opened and read**: the studio's own case study, the product's
  own brand, design or blog page. Strongest.
- **Design press, opened and read**: It's Nice That, PRINT, Transform. Reliable for who did
  what and for quotes, but they mostly restate the studio's own framing.
- **Search summary only**: the page was not opened (blocked, moved or not fetched). Marked
  "not opened" and to be treated as a lead, not a fact.

Two cautions that apply throughout. First, studios describe their own work favorably, and
"warm" is their word; the mechanisms listed here are what they say they did, and the "what
Pollyanna can take" lines are this note's reading, not the sources'. Second, nearly every
identity below uses a proprietary typeface. The typeface section maps each to an open
alternative, and that mapping is a judgment about character, not a claim that the fonts match.

## The short answer

Across the identities, warmth with little clutter comes from the same handful of moves:

1. **One hero color on a quiet ground**, not a palette. Mailchimp, Zocdoc, Headspace and
   Moonpig each own one color and let nearly everything else be near-white and near-black.
2. **One characterful element in a typeface that is otherwise plain.** A smile-shaped curve, a
   leaning letter, a soft terminal. The body text stays highly legible.
3. **A mark that is a face or a figure built from very few shapes**, which can change
   expression or pose. It carries the personality so the interface does not have to.
4. **Illustration that looks made by a hand** (a loose line, an imperfect shape), used
   sparingly, against a strict layout.
5. **Motion that explains where things went**, with decoration saved for rare moments.
6. **Plain, kind words.** In several of these brands the writing does more of the warming
   than the visuals.

Clutter is avoided by rationing: one color, one quirk, one illustration per screen, delight
only where an action is rare. The current look spends its character everywhere at once
(rotation, torn edges, stamps, typewriter type on every line), which is the opposite pattern.

## Identities

### Mailchimp (Collins with the in-house team, 2018)

Sources: [Collins case study](https://wearecollins.com/work/mailchimp) (opened);
[Mailchimp brand assets](https://mailchimp.com/about/brand-assets/) (opened);
[Mailchimp Content Style Guide, voice and tone](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/)
(opened); typeface history from [Fonts In Use](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/39539/mailchimp-identity-2018-redesign)
(not opened).

- **Color:** "Cavendish Yellow is Mailchimp's hero color. We use Peppercorn for accents." One
  color, one dark.
- **Illustration:** Collins says the style "pays homage to the spirit of outsider art,"
  "embracing imperfection and a hand-drawn aesthetic." Rough black drawings sit on flat yellow
  inside a tidy layout.
- **Mark:** a winking head (Freddie) always paired with the wordmark.
- **Type:** Cooper Light at launch, a light soft serif, later replaced by a custom family
  (per Fonts In Use, not opened). A soft old-style serif used large and light reads warm
  without the weight of Cooper Black.
- **Words:** four voice principles: "We are plainspoken," "We are genuine," "We are
  translators," "Our humor is dry." Collins describes the voice as "wry humor, modest
  celebration, and a dash of absurdity."
- **Stated aim:** "a precise balance between the sophisticated and the surreal."
- **What Pollyanna can take:** a single ground color plus ink; rough drawings allowed only
  because everything around them is strict; a light soft serif for headings. Caution: yellow
  works as a ground under dark ink but fails contrast as a text color.

### Headspace (in-house with Italic Studio, type by Colophon, 2024)

Source: [It's Nice That, April 25, 2024](https://itsnicethat.com/articles/italic-studio-headspace-graphic-design-project-250424) (opened).

- **Color:** orange still leads, with a supporting palette meant to "better represent the
  range of human emotions," and pairings adjusted for contrast.
- **Type:** a custom cut of Colophon's Aperçu, a plain grotesque whose curves echo the brand's
  smile, chosen to "flex from playful to clinical."
- **Illustration:** a circle with a face, extended to a range of expressions beyond a smile
  (stress, sadness, contentment).
- **Motion:** animation kept to "help simplify complex ideas."
- **Stated aim:** "to be kind, warm and welcoming."
- **What Pollyanna can take:** the simplest possible figure (a dot, a curve) can hold a whole
  brand if it is allowed several states. The girl seen from behind has no face, so her states
  would have to come from pose, braids and bows, which is a useful constraint to design for.

### Duolingo (in-house; brand by Johnson Banks, 2019)

Sources: [Duolingo blog, "Shape language: Duolingo's art style," July 2, 2020](https://blog.duolingo.com/shape-language-duolingos-art-style/)
(opened). The detailed guideline pages at design.duolingo.com now redirect to the blog hub;
the rules "every shape has rounded edges" and "shadows are a pill shape, not an oval" come
from a search summary of those pages (not opened).

- **Shape:** illustrations are a "few cleverly placed vector shapes," moved from "flat, pointy
  shapes" to "rounder, friendlier illustrations," using "the fewest details needed to get the
  point across."
- **Why:** the style is "quick to produce, clear to understand, and fun to learn with." A
  rule set this small lets one person draw every asset consistently.
- **What Pollyanna can take:** a written shape rule (for example: three base shapes, one
  corner radius, no outlines or one outline weight) keeps a bow, a girl, a gift and empty
  states in one family, and survives at 16px. Caution: this exact look (bright, rounded,
  flat) is now widely copied, so it needs a twist to avoid reading as generic.

### Zocdoc (Wolff Olins, 2016)

Source: [Transform magazine, February 18, 2016](https://www.transformmagazine.net/articles/2016/zee-new-face-of-healthcare/)
(opened). Zocdoc's own page and the Design Week and Fast Company pieces refused the fetch.

- **Mark:** "Zee," a letter Z plus two dots for eyes, forming a face that changes expression.
- **Color:** bright yellow against a sector of blues and greens.
- **Words:** attributes "simple, smart, and caring"; the brief was to be "friendly, simple
  and, most of all, reflective of patients and real life."
- **What Pollyanna can take:** a letterform that doubles as the character. A bow drawn so it
  can sit inside the wordmark (as a letter, a dot or a knot) is one mark instead of two.

### Girl Scouts (Collins, 2019 to 2022)

Source: [Collins case study](https://wearecollins.com/work/girl-scouts) (opened).

- **Mark:** the trefoil, "now simplified, embodies its most potent, iconic essence," and it
  "can now selectively expand beyond green-only applications."
- **Silhouettes removed:** the earlier mark held girls' profiles; Collins dropped them, on
  the grounds that "removing silhouettes enhances inclusivity."
- **Shape:** patches and badges turned into "bold, geometric forms that could be used as
  building blocks for design and interactivity."
- **What Pollyanna can take:** two things. A heritage symbol reduced to a flat shape can take
  any color, which helps a no-single-holiday rule. And a figure that shows a specific face or
  profile narrows who sees themselves in it; the girl seen from behind already avoids a face,
  and the same care applies to hair, skin and build when she is redrawn.

### Moonpig (in-house with F37 Foundry, 2017)

Sources: [F37 case study](https://f37.com/case-studies/moonpig) (opened); the pink color, the
snout app icon and the wordmark animation are from a search summary of
[It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/news/moonpig-rebrand-identity-graphic-design-280917)
and the [D&AD entry](https://www.dandad.org/awards/professional/2018/branding/26741/moonpig-rebrand/)
(not opened).

- **Mascot retired into type:** F37 says the work replaced "our old-fashioned cartoon logo
  with a modern yet fun logotype." The typeface, Moonpig Lift-Off, has alternate letters
  that lift, wobble and shake.
- **A trace of the old mascot kept small:** the pig survives as a snout-shaped icon (not
  opened).
- **What Pollyanna can take:** this is the closest precedent to the task, a cards-and-gifts
  brand that kept its character while removing the cartoon. The character moved into the
  behavior of the letters and one small icon. Notably it is not seasonal.

### Notion (illustration by Roman Muradov)

Source: [Notion blog, July 10, 2024](https://www.notion.com/blog/the-thinking-behind-our-latest-brand-campaign) (opened).

- **Illustration:** minimalist black-and-white drawings with loose, gestural linework, which
  the illustrator says stood out from the vector illustration most companies used. "People
  started telling me they got into Notion primarily because they loved the illustrations."
- **Color:** the product stays nearly colorless; bold yellow, blue and red entered only for
  a campaign.
- **Viewpoint:** early drawings showed full figures at a distance, later ones move closer.
  "Moving closer is a natural part of storytelling."
- **What Pollyanna can take:** a nearly monochrome interface can still be the warm one if
  its drawings are visibly made by a person. This is the strongest counter to flat vector
  sameness, and it suits a figure with no face: the line itself carries the feeling.

### Etsy (Sylvain, with Upstatement for the product system, 2026)

Source: [PRINT, April 29, 2026](https://www.printmag.com/?p=812779) (opened).

- **Idea:** "discovery as a human experience."
- **Mark and type:** the logo reduced to a square, "both container and portal"; a bespoke
  wordmark with generous serif curves and a "distinctive rounded 'y'."
- **Color:** an extended palette built on the existing orange.
- **Voice:** "balances boldness with warmth."
- **What Pollyanna can take:** a soft serif wordmark inside a plain geometric container is a
  current way to say "handmade, human" without texture or paper effects.

### Airbnb Cereal (Airbnb with Dalton Maag, 2018)

Source: [It's Nice That, May 15, 2018](https://itsnicethat.com/news/airbnb-cereal-typeface-font-dalton-maag-graphic-design-150518)
(opened). Airbnb's own article would not load.

- **Type:** one sans family in six weights, with open apertures and a taller x-height, judged
  "through a product design lens" for legibility first.
- **What Pollyanna can take:** warmth in an interface typeface is mostly roundness in the
  bowls and openness in the apertures, not decoration. One family across interface and
  headings is the least cluttered option.

### Material 3 Expressive (Google, 2025)

Source: [Google Design, "Better, Easier, Emotional UX"](https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research) (opened).

- **Means:** "color, shape, size, motion, and containment."
- **Evidence:** 46 studies and "more than 18,000 participants." Key elements were spotted "up
  to four times faster" in the expressive versions, and users aged 45 and over found them as
  fast as younger users.
- **Limits, stated by Google:** "When basic interaction paradigms are broken, expressive
  design can lead to poor usability or negative sentiment." Removing text labels lowered
  usability, and a "strong minority of users" preferred calmer designs.
- **What Pollyanna can take:** this is the only source here with numbers, and it supports
  the owner's complaint from the other side: expression helps when it makes the one important
  action bigger and clearer, and hurts when it replaces familiar patterns or labels. The main
  action on each screen (see who you got, join, draw) should be the largest, most colored
  thing; labels stay as words.

### Family (wallet app; essay by Benji Taylor)

Source: ["Family Values"](https://benji.org/family-values) (opened).

- **Three principles:** simplicity, fluidity, delight.
- **Fluidity:** a button label morphs from "Continue" to "Confirm" by keeping the shared
  letters; elements that persist between steps move instead of being redrawn; tabs slide in
  the direction tapped.
- **Delight is rationed by frequency:** rarely used features get the most celebration (a
  confetti burst after a backup), frequent ones get almost none. The essay calls this
  "selective emphasis."
- **What Pollyanna can take:** the reveal happens once per person per event, which makes it
  the rarest and most deserving moment in the app. By this rule, nearly all of the motion
  budget belongs to untying the bow, and day-to-day screens stay still. Motion must also
  respect a reduced-motion setting and cannot be relied on in email.

### Monzo (tone of voice)

Source: [Monzo tone of voice](https://monzo.com/tone-of-voice) (opened).

- **Principles:** "straightforward kindness" for all writing; "everyday magic" and "warm wit"
  mainly for marketing.
- **Rules:** everyday words over business words; read it aloud and rewrite anything you would
  not say face to face; active voice; apologize once and only when at fault; "serious is not
  formal"; humor as "a pinch, not a dollop."
- **A dial by context:** operational messages get high kindness, low magic and no wit.
- **What Pollyanna can take:** a written dial like this would settle where the interface may
  be playful (empty states, the reveal) and where it must be plain (errors, the draw, sign-in).
  The words of the notification emails are fixed by SPEC.md §15 and are outside this.

### Two print references for the motifs

**Paul Rand's UPS logo (1961).** Source: [paulrand.design](https://www.paulrand.design/life/books-articles/articles/print/2014-TM-The-Untold-Stories.html) (opened).
A string-tied parcel sits on top of a shield. Rand's argument was that the bow was the only
way to make a rectangle read as a parcel, and it suggested gifts. His seven-year-old daughter,
shown the sketch, said "That's a present, Daddy." The bow there is a few plain strokes with
no shading, no ribbon folds and no color, and it still reads at small sizes and belongs to no
holiday. That is a useful test for the redrawn bow: does it read as "a present" in one color
at favicon size?

**Dick Bruna.** Source: [Tokyo Opera City Art Gallery exhibition text](https://operacity.jp/ag/exh190/e/exh02.php) (opened).
Bruna designed roughly 2,000 paperback covers over about 20 years and pursued "simplicity of
expression" and "the strengths of lines and colours on a flat surface," influenced by
Matisse, Léger and De Stijl. His picture books use "black lines and flat colour planes in
just four colours, red, yellow, blue, and green." A slightly uneven black line around flat
color is a 70-year-old look that still reads as modern, and it is a proven way to draw a
child-like figure with almost no detail.

### Two ideas behind the motifs

**The figure seen from behind.** Art history has a name for it, the Rückenfigur, best known
from Caspar David Friedrich. Source: Barbara Hess in [Hatje Cantz, *Caspar David Friedrich
A–Z*](https://hatjecantz.com/blogs/textauszuge-a-z/r-ruckenfigur) (opened). The figure "draws
the gaze into the picture and at the same time obstructs it," works as "a representative and
figure of identification for the viewers," and, because what she sees is hidden, appeals "to
the imagination." Scholars disagree about how far viewers identify with such a figure. For
Pollyanna the reading is apt: she is looking at something the viewer cannot see yet, which is
the situation of everyone before the reveal. A layout that places her facing into the page,
toward the thing to be revealed, uses the motif for what it is good at.

**The bow as a knot, not a decoration.** In Japanese gift wrapping the bow knot
(*chō-musubi*) is used for happy occasions one hopes will come again, because it can be untied
and retied, while knots that cannot be undone mark one-time events. This comes from search
summaries of [Web Japan](https://web-japan.org/trends/11_fashion/fas180104.html) and a
[Japanese culture reference](https://japanese-wiki-corpus.github.io/culture/Mizuhiki.html)
(neither opened; customs vary by region). It is offered as a precedent, not as a look to
borrow: a bow drawn as flat cord, in any color, is a gift sign with no link to a winter
holiday, and "it can be untied" is already the mechanic of the reveal.

## Means, summarized

| Means | What the references do | Works without remote assets? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Color | One hero color on near-white, one dark for text, at most one accent. Pairings checked for contrast (Headspace). | Yes. CSS only. |
| Type | A plain, open sans for reading; warmth from one soft or quirky trait, or from a soft serif used large for headings. | Yes, with self-hosted WOFF2 files. Email falls back to system fonts. |
| Illustration | Few shapes or a loose line; one drawing per screen; a figure with states. | Yes. Inline SVG. |
| Shape | A written rule (base shapes, one radius). Geometric containers around soft content. | Yes. CSS and SVG. |
| Motion | Explains continuity; decoration saved for rare moments. | Yes. CSS transitions and SVG; needs a reduced-motion fallback. |
| Words | Plain, kind, spoken register; humor rationed by context. | Yes. |

## Open-licensed typefaces that carry warmth without looking dated

All of these are under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, checked against the license recorded
on each GitHub repository on November 26, 2026. The OFL allows bundling the font files with
the app and serving them from it; the copyright notice and license text must travel with the
files, as the repo already does for its current fonts
([OFL text and FAQ](https://openfontlicense.org/)). Check each font's Reserved Font Name
before renaming or modifying a font. Descriptions of character are this note's judgment
unless quoted.

**Soft serifs, for headings and a wordmark**

- **Fraunces** ([repo and license](https://github.com/undercasetype/Fraunces)). A "soft-serif"
  old-style display face inspired by Windsor, Souvenir and the Cooper series, with variable
  axes for softness and "wonk" (leaning, quirky letters). Google's commissioning designer
  "aimed to publish a variable font that had warmth and a sense of humor"
  ([Google Design](https://design.google/library/wonky-goofy-fraunces-typeface/), opened).
  The nearest open relative of Mailchimp's Cooper Light. Caution: it became very popular
  after 2020, and a soft serif on cream with a coral accent is the exact look §12 lists
  under "Avoid."
- **Young Serif** ([repo and license](https://github.com/noirblancrouge/YoungSerif)). A heavier
  old-style serif, one weight, sturdy and bookish. Less common than Fraunces.
- **Instrument Serif** ([repo and license](https://github.com/Instrument/instrument-serif)).
  A condensed display serif, elegant more than cozy. Regular and italic only.
- **Newsreader** ([repo and license](https://github.com/productiontype/Newsreader)) and
  **Literata** ([repo and license](https://github.com/googlefonts/literata)). Text serifs
  built for on-screen reading, calmer than the three above.

**Warm sans families, for the interface**

- **Figtree** ([repo and license](https://github.com/erikdkennedy/figtree)). "A friendly,
  simple geometric sans serif font" by its own description. Clean with slightly rounded
  joins.
- **Bricolage Grotesque** ([repo and license](https://github.com/ateliertriay/bricolage)).
  "An expressive variable font with weight, width and optical size axis." Quirky at display
  sizes, plainer at text sizes, so one family can do both jobs.
- **Atkinson Hyperlegible Next** ([repo and license](https://github.com/googlefonts/atkinson-hyperlegible-next);
  [Braille Institute](https://www.brailleinstitute.org/freefont/), opened). Drawn for readers
  with low vision: distinct letter pairs, large open counters, seven weights. Its odd,
  deliberate letter shapes give it character, and it supports the contrast and legibility
  goals directly.
- **Nunito** ([repo and license](https://github.com/googlefonts/nunito)). Rounded terminals.
  Friendly, but widely used in app templates since the mid-2010s, so it risks looking generic.
- **Gabarito** ([repo and license](https://github.com/naipefoundry/gabarito)) and
  **DM Sans** ([repo and license](https://github.com/googlefonts/dm-fonts)). Geometric and
  round. Gabarito is the chunkier, more playful of the two.
- **Instrument Sans** ([repo and license](https://github.com/Instrument/instrument-sans)) and
  **Hanken Grotesk** ([repo and license](https://github.com/marcologous/hanken-grotesk)).
  Neutral grotesques with a little softness, for a direction that keeps type quiet.
- **Google Sans Flex** ([repo and license](https://github.com/googlefonts/googlesans-flex)).
  Released under the OFL in November 2025 (release date and its roundness axis are from a
  search summary, not opened). Caution: it is strongly associated with Google products.
- **Recursive** ([repo and license](https://github.com/arrowtype/recursive)). Has a "casual"
  axis that moves from strict to brush-like in one file, plus a monospace. An option if any
  typewriter flavor should survive.

**Hands, for the revealed name**

- **Shantell Sans** ([repo and license](https://github.com/arrowtype/shantell-sans)). "A
  marker-style font built for creative expression, typographic play, and animation," with
  axes for bounce and informality (axis details from a search summary, not opened). A modern
  replacement for the pencil hand, and it can animate.
- **Playpen Sans** ([repo and license](https://github.com/TypeTogether/Playpen-Sans)). A tidy
  handwriting face from TypeTogether with many alternates so repeated letters differ.

For reference, the current **Courier Prime** is also OFL
([repo and license](https://github.com/quoteunquoteapps/CourierPrime)).

Every font file is a download the page must make from the app, so a direction should name at
most two families and prefer variable files.

## Direction seeds

Four starting points for the prototype ticket, chosen to differ from one another on purpose.
Each names its references, what the bow and the girl become, and its main risk. They are
seeds, not proposals.

### Seed 1: One color, one ribbon

- **References:** Mailchimp, Zocdoc, Headspace, Rand's UPS bow.
- **Look:** a single warm ground or hero color with near-black ink and white surfaces. One
  plain, open sans (Figtree or Atkinson Hyperlegible Next). No texture, no rotation.
- **Bow:** a few flat strokes of cord in one contrasting color used for nothing else, small
  enough to live inside the wordmark.
- **Girl:** a flat silhouette in ink, used once, in the masthead.
- **Statuses:** plain words with a dot or a weight change.
- **Risk:** closest to a generic app. It depends entirely on the color choice, the wordmark
  and the writing to be distinct.

### Seed 2: Drawn by hand, set in type

- **References:** Notion, Bruna, Etsy's wordmark, Mailchimp's illustration.
- **Look:** a nearly colorless page, a soft serif for headings (Fraunces or Young Serif) over
  a quiet sans, and loose ink-line drawings, with one flat color filling the bows only.
- **Bow and girl:** a single continuous-looking line, visibly hand-drawn, with the girl
  facing into the page toward the content. Several poses for different states.
- **Statuses:** small caps or italic serif words, no containers.
- **Risk:** drifts toward the cream, serif and coral look that §12 lists under "Avoid" unless
  the ground stays cool or white and the accent is not coral. It also needs real drawing
  skill; a weak line looks worse than no illustration.

### Seed 3: Cut shapes

- **References:** Duolingo's shape language, Girl Scouts' badge shapes, Bruna's flat color
  planes, Material 3 Expressive.
- **Look:** three or four flat, saturated colors that do not form a holiday pairing, big
  rounded geometric blocks as containers, and a chunky sans (Bricolage Grotesque or
  Gabarito).
- **Bow:** two rounded shapes and a knot, a building block that can tile, scale and become a
  pattern. **Girl:** the same shapes, head, two braids, two bows, no outline.
- **Statuses:** a shape and a color per state, always with the word.
- **Risk:** the most likely to feel busy again if the color count grows, and bright rounded
  shapes are widely copied. Google's own research warns that expression which replaces
  familiar patterns lowers usability.

### Seed 4: Quiet page, one moment

- **References:** Family's "selective emphasis," Material 3 Expressive's findings on the key
  action, Monzo's tone dial, Moonpig's character moved into behavior.
- **Look:** almost no decoration. A neutral, generous layout, a neutral grotesque (Instrument
  Sans or Hanken Grotesk), one accent color reserved for the single main action on each
  screen.
- **Bow:** small and still everywhere except the reveal, where it is large and all of the
  motion happens: the untying, then a hand-set name (Shantell Sans). **Girl:** only on the
  home screen and in empty states.
- **Warmth:** carried mostly by the words and by that one moment.
- **Risk:** between reveals it may read as cold, and its best feature does not appear in
  screenshots, email or a favicon.

The seeds can be crossed. The most natural crosses are 1 with 4 (one color plus a rationed
reveal) and 2 with 4 (hand line plus a quiet page).

## What this research did not settle

- Whether any of this survives in email. The earlier email research on the
  `research/email-client-support` branch applies; web fonts and motion cannot be assumed
  there.
- Actual palettes. No hex values are proposed here; any hero color has to be tested for
  WCAG AA against its ink and its ground.
- Dark mode, which §12 currently rules out and map #50 leaves open.
- Sources that could not be opened: Zocdoc's own page, Airbnb's own typeface article, Design
  Week and Fast Company on Zocdoc, Duolingo's original guideline pages, and Web Japan on
  gift knots.
